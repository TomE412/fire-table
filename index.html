How to use this guide

Do the steps in order. Each step says what to do, then gives a Check. Don't move on until the check is right. Tick Done as you finish each step; it stays ticked on this phone.

All sizes are in millimetres (mm). Every cut is straight across (square). There are no angled cuts in this table.

Tools: grinder with cutting and flap discs, welder (arc or MIG), drill with 6, 9 and 13 mm bits, tape measure, steel square, 4+ clamps, marker, a flat concrete floor. Packers: a few 10 mm thick offcuts to use as spacers.

STEP 1Buy the materials

  • Angle iron 40 × 40 × 3: 3 lengths of 6 m
  • Flat bar 50 × 6: 1 m
  • Round bar 12 mm: 1 m
  • Steel pipe ½ inch (21 mm outside): a 100 mm offcut
  • Teak planks 1200 × 100 × 20: 11
  • Bolts M12 × 70, nyloc nut, 2 washers: 2 sets (hinges)
  • Bolts M12 × 40, nyloc nut, 2 washers: 2 sets (middle pivots)
  • Thick M12 washers (2–3 mm): 2
  • Cup-head bolts M8 × 45, nyloc nut, washer: 28 sets (slats)
  • Fire bin: laser-cut from 2 mm plate (send the files in the Laser files tab to a laser cutter)
  • Zinc primer, enamel paint, high-temperature spray paint

STEP 3Drill the holes

Drill a 6 mm pilot hole first, then open it up to full size. Clean off the burrs.

Check: lay all 4 legs side by side, TOP ends level. Every hole should line up.

STEP 4Make the top frame

Build it upside-down on a flat floor. The top faces all sit on the floor, so they come out perfectly level.

  1. Lay the two A rails on the floor, flat side down, 1200 long, with the upright sides on the inside facing each other. The holes are in the flat side.
  2. Put the two B end pieces between the rails at each end, flat side down, upright side on the outside. Clamp. The frame is 1200 × 1000 outside.
  3. Measure both diagonals (corner to corner). Tap the frame until they're the same. Tack all four corners. Measure again.
  4. Put the two C bin bearers between the rails, flat side down, upright sides facing each other. Measure 390 from each end to the outside of C. The gap between them is 340. Use a D piece as a spacer to check.
  5. Put the two D slat supports between the C bearers, flat side down, upright on the side nearest the rail, 65 from the rail. Holes in the flat side.
  6. Put the two E bin supports between the C bearers, sitting on 10 mm packers (so they end up 10 mm lower than the top), upright side nearest D, with a 10 mm gap to D.
  7. Tack everything. Check the diagonals one last time, then weld all joints on both sides of each corner.
  8. Weld the two J hinge plates to the inside of the A rails' upright sides, both at the hinge end. The hole centre is 150 from the end. The square end of J sits on the floor (level with the top of the rail) and the rounded end with the hole sticks up. Weld along both sides and the top of J where it touches the rail.

Check: diagonals equal (within 2 mm). Turn it over: the top is flat with no weld bumps where slats will sit. Grind any bumps flat.

STEP 5Make leg frame A (the outer one)

  1. Lay two F legs on the floor, flat side down, TOP ends level, with the upright sides on the inside facing each other.
  2. Put a G bar (824) between the legs, flat side down, 60 down from the top, with its upright edge towards the top. It should fit snugly between the two upright sides.
  3. Put the second G bar at the bottom, flush with the bottom ends, upright edge at the bottom.
  4. The outside width should now be 904. Check the diagonals are equal, then tack, check again, and weld.

Check: the frame lies flat on the floor without rocking. The hole at the top (hinge) and the middle hole (pivot) are clear and not covered by weld.

STEP 6Make leg frame B (the inner one) with the axle

  1. Lay the other two F legs flat side down, TOP ends level, with the upright sides on the outside this time.
  2. Push the L axle through both top holes. Centre it so the same amount (about 49) sticks out each side. The outside width of the legs is 820.
  3. Lay a H bar (814) on top of the legs' flat sides, between the two upright sides, 60 down from the top. Put the second H at the bottom, flush with the bottom ends.
  4. Check the diagonals, tack, check again, weld. Then weld the axle to both legs, all the way round on both sides.
  5. Hold frame B against the top frame: the axle must fit between the two A rails with about 1 mm to spare each side. Grind the ends if it's tight.

Check: frame B slides inside frame A with a small gap at each side.

STEP 7Bolt the legs together and hang them

  1. Slide frame B inside frame A. Line up the middle holes. Push an M12 × 40 bolt through A's leg, then a thick washer, then B's leg, then a washer and nyloc nut. Do both sides.
  2. Tighten until snug, then back off a little. The frames must scissor freely.
  3. Turn the top frame right way up and prop it about 750 off the floor.
  4. Hang frame A from the J hinge plates. Each hinge: M12 × 70 bolt, head behind J (under the rail), through J, then the M spacer, then A's top hole, washer and nyloc nut.

Check: frame A swings freely on the hinges, and the axle on frame B reaches up under the rails at the other end.

STEP 8Mark and cut the notches

The notches hold the axle at each height. Mark them from the real table so the top comes out level.

  1. Clamp a K plate to the inside of each A rail at the slide end, 15 below the top of the rail. Its far end is 50 short of the end of the rail.
  2. Set the table to the first height. Measure from the floor to the top of the steel: 730 (the 20 mm slats bring it to 750). Check it's level both ways.
  3. Mark the centre of the axle on both K plates.
  4. Repeat for 580 (table 600) and 430 (table 450).
  5. Unclamp K. At each mark, drill a 13 mm hole and cut down to the bottom edge (see the picture).
  6. Clamp K back in place. Check the axle drops into every notch on both sides and the top is level. Then weld K: three short welds along the top edge.

The marks should come out roughly 750, 860 and 935 from the hinge hole. If yours are very different, recheck the heights.

STEP 9Put the fire bin together

  1. Send the files in the Laser files tab to a laser cutter. Material: 2 mm mild steel. Ask them to bend the big piece (P1) on the red line into a V.
  2. Push the four end tabs of the V (P1) through the slots in the two end plates (P2).
  3. Drop the rim ring (P3) on top. The tabs come up through its slots.
  4. Clamp it square. Tack each tab with a short tack only, because 2 mm plate burns through easily. Add a short weld at each bottom corner.

Check: the bin drops into the frame onto the two E supports and lifts out easily by the hand slots.

STEP 10Clean up and paint

  1. Take the legs off. Grind all sharp edges and corners smooth.
  2. Zinc primer, then enamel, on the frame and legs.
  3. High-temperature paint on the E bin supports and the whole fire bin.
  4. Bolt the legs back on once the paint is dry.

STEP 11Fit the teak slats

  1. Cut 3 of the planks in two, each piece 260 long. That gives 6 short pieces. Keep the other 8 full length.
  2. Lay the slats across the frame, starting flush with the hinge end: full, full, full, full, short, short, short, full, full, full, full. The short pieces go at both sides of the bin.
  3. Use 10 mm packers for even 10 mm gaps. The slats overhang the rails by 100 on each side.
  4. From underneath, drill up through each hole in the steel with a 9 mm bit into the slat above.
  5. Fit cup-head M8 bolts from the top, with a washer and nyloc nut underneath. Don't crush the wood.

STEP 12Final checks

  • Set it at 750, 600 and 450. The top is level each time and doesn't rock.
  • Lift the bin out, then fold it flat. The legs must not touch the slats.
  • No sharp edges anywhere a hand or leg could catch.
  • Finished weight about 60 kg, about 52 kg with the bin lifted out.

Safety: never fold or move the table with a fire in the bin. The bin and its supports get very hot.
